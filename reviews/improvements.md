# Review: Areas for Improvement

> Feedback received May 2026 on the first draft of *The Geometry of Meaning*.

---

## 1. Length and Depth Balance

The manuscript is currently quite short for a "book-length" work. Chapters 5 and 6 are the most substantial; earlier chapters could benefit from one or two additional concrete examples or small thought experiments. Chapter 7 (Multivector Hypothesis) feels more speculative and would benefit from at least one worked example of a multivector word representation.

## 2. Missing Bridge Between GA and Language

While the connection to 3D geometry (GATr, GAFL, CGA) is well covered, the leap to language remains somewhat abstract. The manuscript would be stronger with a short section showing how a specific linguistic phenomenon (e.g., compositionality, negation, or analogy) maps more naturally to geometric products or bivectors than to vector operations.

## 3. Underdeveloped "Multivector Embedding Hypothesis"

Chapter 7 introduces an exciting idea but stops short of making it concrete. Adding even a small table or diagram showing how different grades might correspond to linguistic features (scalar = part-of-speech, bivector = semantic relation, etc.) would make the hypothesis more tangible.

## 4. Tone and Audience Calibration

The writing occasionally oscillates between "curious general reader" and "researcher already familiar with the projects." Some sentences assume knowledge of the repos that a new reader won't have. A light pass to ensure every technical claim is motivated before it appears would help.

## 5. Visual and Structural Polish

- More diagrams (even simple ones) would help, especially for rotors, the sandwich product, and multivector grades.
- The chapter titles are good, but some subsections in Chapter 5 feel list-like.
- The closing of Chapter 8 is strong, but the manuscript ends rather abruptly after the personal note.

## Specific Recommendations

- Add 1–2 short worked examples in Chapter 7 showing a multivector interaction.
- Expand the discussion of CliffordFrameAttention (currently buried in the roadmap) into its own short section or sidebar.
- Consider adding a one-page "Glossary of Geometric Objects" (scalar, vector, bivector, rotor, geometric product) early in the book.
- In Chapter 6, make the connection between the three projects more explicit with a small diagram or table showing data flow or shared primitives.
- Add a short "Limitations and Open Questions" subsection somewhere in Chapter 8 to increase credibility.
